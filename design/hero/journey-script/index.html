One brand, one day, six stops.

The visitor scrolls and our character flies through one day in the life of a single brand, from sunrise on a coast road to a city square at night. At each stop it finds one kind of our work, out in the real world. Nothing of ours appears on the way between stops.

The brandTorvi, our own cycle brand, at every stop. No client names needed.
LengthAbout 9.6 screens of scroll on a desktop, about 8 on a phone.

Every stop plays the same three beats

  1. Arrive. The camera slows and settles. The character swings round from looking ahead to looking at the work.
  2. Show. The heading, one line and the button fade in on the side away from the work. The marked objects start to respond to the pointer.
  3. Hold. If the visitor stops scrolling, the character turns to face them. After six seconds it glances at the work and back, and a small Keep scrolling hint appears.

The travel look

Between stops the journey should feel fast, as in the reference picture you sent.

  • Speed in the scenery. The place blurs and streaks in the direction of travel, people and plants included. The faster the scroll, the longer the streaks. The character itself stays sharp.
  • The character at an angle. We see it from behind and slightly to one side, eye pointing where it is going, leaning into the turn.
  • Light tail in our colours. A wide beam streams from its back toward us and fades out: cyan, blue, violet, pink and orange, the same order as the line on its body. No green and no yellow.
  • Light on the ground. The same colours wash softly over the ground under the tail, so the tail belongs to the place and is not pasted on top.
  • Slowing down. As a stop comes near, the streaks shorten, the tail thins to nothing and the picture comes into focus.
  • Animations turned off. No blur, no tail; the scenes change with a plain fade.

How it stays fast and findable

  • The first screen is the sunrise picture and the heading, sent as ordinary page content. That is what PageSpeed measures, so the score does not wait for 3D.
  • The character and the 3D objects load after the first screen is up, outside the page's first-load budget. The character's file needs slimming from about 2.4 MB to a few hundred KB first.
  • Each scene's picture loads one stop ahead, never all at once.
  • The main heading stays the page's only h1. Each stop's heading is an h2 with real text and a real link, so search engines read all six services.

Order of work

  1. The journey in plain colours. The full scroll with the real character, words, timings and buttons, on flat sky-coloured scenes. Free. You feel the pacing before anything is spent.
  2. One stop for real: the booth. One generated picture and the 3D booth. This proves that a 3D object sits believably in a photographed place.
  3. The other five stops and the road. Six more pictures and four sets of objects.
  4. Phone pass. Crops, sizes and touch at 390px.
  5. Into the site. Slim the files, check PageSpeed on phone and desktop, then replace the glass-studio hero.

The one real risk. A 3D object placed in a generated photo can look pasted on. Step 2 tests exactly that with the booth before more credits go out. If it does not hold up, the fallback is a generated picture of each object with a gentle shift of a few degrees instead of a full look-round.

Choices made here, yours to change

  • One brand all the way. Torvi at every stop, so the journey reads as one story and no client is named on Home.
  • Six stops. Brand, Websites and apps, Print, Automation, Social and marketing, Video. The creative subscription is the closing line, not a stop.
  • All the words are drafts. Headings, lines and button labels are first proposals for you to edit.
  • Length. About 9.6 screens. Shorter legs would bring it down to about 8 without losing a stop.
